## What is and is not certified

The new development verifies concrete boundary geometry, the Fueter action, its L² construction conditional on the area formula, strong continuity, Kelvin conjugation, and the research deductions conditional on explicitly stated analytic and spectral inputs. It also proves the degree-one specialization of SC3 covariance, the constant-vector Hardy norm, the diagonal compact fibre, and the exact hyperbolic weight.

The highest-level statement is `MathResearch.Hardy.Hardy_pi1_of_background`. It constructs a unitary intertwiner from the concrete Hardy boundary space to an **externally supplied representation** π₁, assuming the five named inputs below. `Hardy_endpoint_realization_of_inputs` additionally records the positive and negative irreducibility deductions, continuity, orthogonal totality, and the finite-radial-norm trace realization.

This is **not an unconditional verification of the n=1 endpoint**. No theorem supplies the five open inputs. No general definition of “limit of discrete series,” no Fell topology, and no proof of the external classification has been encoded. In particular, the term “limit” is not defined here to mean irreducible, non-square-integrable, or having a particular label. The kernel statement is unitary equivalence to a supplied π₁. Its interpretation as the classified limit requires the external theorem and convention bridge described below.

No complete wiki claim/version is certified by this milestone. The non-square-integrability calculation, the full p>2 coefficient integrability assertion, inequivalence of the two summands, the central-cover assertion, and the exterior-function interpretation are not claimed as formalized here. The boundary Kelvin involution is proved, but that alone is not the exterior regularity theorem. The full induced-section model is also not constructed.

## Definitions a reader can compare

`Hardy/Fiber.lean` uses the existing degree-one binary homogeneous polynomial space with its verified Hermitian norm. For p=aX+bY, `toQuaternion p=a-jb`. This map is bijective and norm preserving. The theorem `toQuaternion_smul` verifies Q(z·p)=Q(p)z; thus the complex structure is the written **right** complex scalar convention. `toQuaternion_action` verifies that the degree-one polynomial action is left quaternion multiplication.

`Hardy/Space.lean` defines `IsRegular f` by smoothness on the open unit ball and the ordinary left Fueter equation on Q∘f. Smooth regularity is the convention already used in the project's Fueter definitions. No new equivalence with a weaker C¹ or distributional formulation is asserted here. Values outside B are immaterial; equalities identifying functions are stated only on B. `IsHardy f` imposes a uniform bound on the spherical square integral for 0<r<1. `hardyNormSq` is the supremum of those integrals. The probability measure is the existing normalized Haar measure on the actual unit quaternions S³.

`IsPolynomial` means polynomial dependence on all four real quaternion coordinates, with two complex polynomial coefficient functions. `hardyBoundary` is the closed complex span in actual Haar L² of traces of continuous regular polynomials. It is not defined as an abstract cyclic representation or as whichever space has the desired classification. `IsBoundaryTrace` is the actual L² radial-limit condition. Nonzero constants belong to both the function and boundary models, with squared norm exactly ‖v‖².

`boundaryAction` uses the matrix of g⁻¹ and exactly |J|⁻²J⁻¹f(q′). `boundaryRepresentation_formula` verifies the ordinary quaternion formula almost everywhere on S³. `BoundaryAreaFormula` uses the density |J|⁻⁶. The norm-squared multiplier is proved to be exactly this density, not an unspecified positive factor.

## Written proof correspondence

1. **Degree one and conformal covariance.** `Fiber.lean` proves the coordinate/norm/scalar identification. `Regularity.lean` proves `fueterCoefficient_one` and invokes the previously checked `SC3_representation_covariance_v1` at n=1. It does not replace SC3 by a different proof. The closed-ball denominator bound and continuity justify the boundary restriction.
2. **Boundary unitarity.** `BoundaryGeometry.lean` constructs the sphere homeomorphism; `BoundaryAction.lean` proves the cocycle and multiplier formulas. `Analysis/BoundaryAreaFormula.lean` states the open surface change-of-variables theorem and derives its integral and integrability consequences using mathlib's map/withDensity theorems. `BoundaryL2.lean` uses those consequences to descend the action to L² and prove its norm, group law and complex linearity.
3. **Density, regularity and invariance.** `Analysis/RegularPolynomialApproximation.lean` separately states the uniform boundary approximation consequence of homogeneous expansion. `Invariance.lean` applies it to a polynomial's transform, which is regular by SC3 and continuous on the closed ball. The resulting invariant span extends by continuity to its closure. `Continuity.lean` proves strong continuity on continuous functions on compact S³ and extends through mathlib's dense-continuous-class theorem and the L² isometries. This expands the internal density step; it does not replace the internal compact-support argument on the noncompact ball from DS2.
4. **Trace identification.** The outputs still required from homogeneous harmonic expansion and Parseval are precisely the fields of `HardyTraceData`: bounded evaluations, separation of vectors, smooth harmonic reconstruction, radial trace, norm equality, realization of every finite-radial-norm regular function, constant evaluations, and compatibility of evaluation with C7. No proof of these fields is claimed. Their statements explicitly include the local trace-equivariance bridge, not just the abstract harmonic expansion theorem.
5. **Irreducibility.** `Irreducible.lean` follows the internal compact-average argument. It invokes the separately proved spherical mean-value theorem to obtain the constant projection; uses a ball-section translate to detect a nonzero value at zero; invokes the existing degree-one compact polynomial irreducibility theorem; and calls the general compact-average/orthogonal-complement lemma. No classification theorem or unrelated irreducibility argument is substituted.
6. **The normalized parameter.** `CompactFormula.lean` proves Π(a,b)f(q)=τ₁(b)f(a⁻¹qb), and identifies the diagonal fibre at 1 as the standard complex two-dimensional representation. `Normalization.lean` constructs the exact a_t with cosh(t/2), sinh(t/2), proves that it fixes 1, and checks Π(a_t) there has factor exp(3t/2). This confirms the stated weight. It does not prove the full MAN decomposition, root convention, induced-section equivalence, or Spin₀(4,1) group isomorphism; those bridges remain part of the external recognition obligation.
7. **Compact spectrum and splitting.** `SpinorCompactExpansionData` states the open Peter–Weyl/Clebsch–Gordan specialization and the local Fueter K-type identification using actual mathlib tensor products V_m⊗V_(m+1), actual compact actions and isometric intertwiners. It includes orthogonality, positive closed span, and full boundary totality. `Splitting.lean` derives the orthogonal Hilbert sum from those data. The statement does not pretend that naming these input fields proves the spectral theorems.
8. **Kelvin and the negative summand.** `Kelvin.lean` proves C is a complex-linear unitary involution using the already proved Haar inversion theorem. `BlockSwap.lean` constructs g↦WgW as an automorphism of the actual matrix group and proves CΠ(g)C=Π(WgW) by the written denominator/numerator calculation on |q|=1. `KelvinIntertwining.lean` descends this identity to L². `Negative.lean` transports the positive representation and irreducibility along that automorphism and verifies that its action agrees with the ambient boundary action. The compact factors are exchanged in `negativeCompact_equivariant`.
9. **External recognition.** `Endpoint.lean` invokes `SpinorEndpointClassification` at the recognition step. This interface says that an irreducible Hilbert realization whose equivariant image is the positive compact spectral summand is unitarily equivalent to supplied π₁. It is an external classification-plus-convention-bridge input, quantified over realizations; it is not an axiom declaration or a theorem proved by this repository. The Hardy realization supplies irreducibility, equivariance and the specified image from the preceding steps.

These are expanded justifications of the supplied route. No alternative proof replaces the canonical route in this milestone. However, the open inputs mean that proof correspondence is incomplete at exactly those steps; a clean kernel build does not close them. This is author correspondence analysis, not independent review.

## Five explicit open inputs and separate exposition needs

- `BoundaryAreaFormula`: map of |J|⁻⁶dσ under the boundary Möbius map equals dσ. A separate exposition should prove the three-dimensional boundary Jacobian and area change of variables. The current Lean file proves only consequences of the stated formula.
- `RegularPolynomialBoundaryApproximation`: regular functions continuous on the closed ball have uniform boundary approximants by genuine regular polynomials. The intended proof is the internal homogeneous-expansion route: first dilate, then truncate; do not silently substitute a different approximation theorem.
- `HardyTraceData`: the harmonic expansion/Parseval Hardy trace theorem and its local Fueter/evaluation/C7 bridges. The required reconstruction, norm and trace statements are visible in its fields. A separate article should give the homogeneous expansion theorem, with the Fueter specialization explicitly distinguished from the general harmonic result.
- `SpinorCompactExpansionData`: Peter–Weyl, Clebsch–Gordan, and the Fueter homogeneous polynomial K-type identification. Separate background expositions should state the actual representations and completeness/orthogonality assertions, not only dimensions. None of these missing theorems was rederived inside the research proof.
- `SpinorEndpointClassification`: recognition of the positive normalized-zero spinor summand as supplied π₁, including the correspondence with the source's induced model and conventions. The classification itself may remain external, but its scope and the local identification bridge must remain explicit.

The cited sources are Liu–Zhang, printed p.2996 after Theorem 3.11, and Camporesi–Pedon, *Harmonic analysis for spinors on real hyperbolic spaces*, Theorem 3.2 final step, printed p.252. The [publisher PDF](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/87388) was inspected at that step: the even-dimensional hyperbolic spinor constituents are classified as limits, with reference to Thieleker's Theorem 6 and Knapp XII.7. This source inspection does not verify Thieleker's proof or the quaternionic coordinate bridge. The retained project PDFs and source references remain unchanged.
